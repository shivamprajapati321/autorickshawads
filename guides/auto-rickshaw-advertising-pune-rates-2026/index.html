Why our pricing looks different from a monthly rate

Most Pune rate cards you will find quote auto rickshaw branding as a monthly rental — a figure per rickshaw per month, billed for the length of the campaign. Ours is a one-time cost, and the difference is not a discount trick. It comes from the fact that we manufacture the hood.

A hood is a physical object. We print it on 500–600 GSM rexine, stitch it, and fit it to the rickshaw. It is built to stay presentable through roughly six months of daily Pune sun, dust and monsoon. You pay once for that object and its installation. There is no second invoice in month two.

When you compare quotes, this is the number that matters: total rupees to get the branding on the road and keep it there for the campaign. A monthly rate of ₹550 across six months is ₹3,300 per rickshaw. Our ₹499 is the whole cost for the same period. Coverage

Which Pune areas to choose

Pune is not one market. A rickshaw working the Hinjewadi shift carries a completely different audience from one working Sadashiv Peth. Below is how we group the city when planning a campaign, and which advertisers each group tends to suit.

The IT corridor — Hinjewadi, Wakad, Baner, Balewadi, Aundh, Pashan, Sus

Rajiv Gandhi Infotech Park and the residential belt feeding it. Heavy commuter volume at fixed hours, a young salaried audience, high smartphone use and strong response to QR codes. Works for fintech, food and quick-commerce apps, gyms and fitness, co-working, consumer electronics, real estate in Baner–Balewadi, and anything with an app install as the goal. Traffic here is slow at peak, which means long dwell time in front of a hood.

The eastern belt — Kharadi, Viman Nagar, Wagholi, Yerwada, Magarpatta, Hadapsar

The second IT and corporate cluster, plus airport-adjacent traffic through Viman Nagar and Yerwada. Similar audience to Hinjewadi with more mixed residential density around Wagholi and Hadapsar. Suits hospitals and diagnostics, banking and insurance, education, real estate, and retail launches.

Central and student Pune — Kothrud, Karve Nagar, Warje, Deccan, FC Road, Shivajinagar, Sadashiv Peth, Swargate

The densest, most walkable part of the city, and the coaching and college heartland. This is where Marathi creative earns its keep. Suits coaching and test-prep institutes, colleges and universities, jewellery and apparel retail, local services, healthcare and clinics. The June–August admission season makes this the most competitive window in Pune for education advertisers.

Camp, Koregaon Park and the old city

Retail, restaurants, hospitality and a mixed local and visitor audience. Shorter trips, denser stops, high frequency of exposure in a small radius. Suits restaurants and cafes, apparel and lifestyle retail, events, and anything with a physical location to drive footfall to.

South Pune — Katraj, Dhankawadi, Bibwewadi, Sinhagad Road, NIBM, Undri

Fast-growing residential Pune with a family-heavy audience. Suits schools, hospitals, home services, FMCG, consumer durables, banking and gold loans, and real estate.

PCMC — Pimpri, Chinchwad, Nigdi, Akurdi, Bhosari, Ravet, Punawale, Moshi, Chakan, Talegaon

The industrial and manufacturing belt, with a large working population and its own retail centres. PCMC is a genuinely separate market from PMC, not a suburb of it, and a campaign that ignores it is leaving a large audience out. Suits automotive and two-wheeler brands, building materials and hardware, industrial suppliers, banking and finance, healthcare, and FMCG.

How many areas should one campaign cover? For most first campaigns in Pune we recommend concentrating on two or three of these groups rather than spreading thinly across all six. A brand seen five times by the right commuter does more than one seen once by five different people. Frequency beats reach at small volumes; once you are past roughly 500 rickshaws, spreading starts to make sense.

Format

Hood or back panel — which to pick in Pune

They are different media, not two price points for the same thing.

The hood is the canopy over the driver. It is visible from the front and from both sides, which means it works on pedestrians, on people in adjacent lanes, and on anyone waiting at a signal. It is the larger canvas and the one that carries a brand message. At ₹499 with a 200 minimum, it is the format for brand campaigns, launches and anything where you need the name to register.

The back panel is the rear vinyl, facing the traffic behind. Its audience is drivers and riders stuck behind the rickshaw — which, in Pune traffic, is a long and attentive exposure. Because the viewer is stationary and close, this is the best format for a QR code, a phone number or a short offer. At ₹150 with a 500 minimum, a back panel campaign covers far more rickshaws for the same money.

A practical way to decide: if the goal is recall, buy hoods. If the goal is a scan or a call, buy back panels. If the budget allows both, run hoods in your two priority areas and back panels city-wide.

Measurement

What reach actually means — and how we calculate it

You will see very large impression numbers quoted for auto rickshaw advertising, usually without any explanation of where they came from. Here is our arithmetic, stated openly so you can disagree with it.

A working auto rickshaw in Pune covers roughly 150 km a day. We estimate roughly 10 people in a position to see the branding per kilometre — pedestrians, other vehicles, people at signals and stops. That gives about 1,500 views per rickshaw per day.

On that basis, a 200-rickshaw hood campaign in Pune produces in the region of 300,000 views a day, or roughly 90 lakh over a month. Our reach calculator runs exactly this formula, and you can change the inputs.

Two honest caveats. First, these are views, not unique people — the same commuter on the same route sees your branding repeatedly, which is good for recall but means you should not read the number as audience size. Second, the 10-per-kilometre figure is an estimate. It will be conservative in Deccan at 7pm and generous on the Chakan road at noon. We publish the assumption rather than the conclusion so you can judge it yourself.

What we can verify absolutely is the count: how many rickshaws carry your branding, and where they were fitted. That comes with GPS-stamped photographs of every unit.

Operations

Permissions, artwork and installation

Permissions are included

You do not need to arrange anything. Permissions for running branded rickshaws in Pune and PCMC are handled by us and are included in the quoted rate. There is no separate permit line on the invoice and no paperwork on your side.

Artwork — any language, designed in-house

We handle all artwork. English, Marathi, Hindi or bilingual panels, and we do the design in-house, so getting the Marathi version typeset correctly costs you nothing extra.

On language strategy in Pune: Marathi and English both perform, and the split is geographic. Kothrud, the peths, Katraj and much of PCMC respond well to Marathi. Hinjewadi, Kharadi, Baner and Viman Nagar skew English. Bilingual panels are the safe default for a city-wide campaign.

On design, the constraint is the medium. A hood is read at a distance, in traffic, often at an angle, by someone who has two or three seconds. One headline, one proof point, one action. That is the formula that survives. If the first proof does not read well at road distance, we change it before anything is fitted — that check happens by default, not on request.

Installation timeline

Installation time depends on volume and on how spread out the areas are. A concentrated 200-rickshaw campaign in two adjacent areas fits far faster than the same 200 spread from Chakan to Katraj. Because printing and stitching happen at our own Pune unit, there is no transport lead time in the middle of the process.

We will give you a dated installation schedule with your quote rather than a vague promise, and you will see the fitting progress in the GPS-stamped photos as they come in.

Planning

Planning a Pune campaign by budget

Rough guide to what different budgets buy in Pune, all figures before GST.

Around ₹1 lakh — 200 hoods. The entry point. Concentrate on one or two areas. Right for a local launch, a single-location business, or a first test of the medium before committing further.

Around ₹75,000 — 500 back panels. Wider physical spread than the 200-hood option and better suited to a QR-code or phone-number response campaign. Good for app installs and offer-led retail.

Around ₹2.5 lakh — 500 hoods. Enough to cover three or four area groups with real frequency in each. This is where a city-wide Pune presence starts to feel city-wide.

₹5 lakh and above — 1,000+ hoods, or a hood and back panel combination. At this level we would normally split formats: hoods for brand presence in priority areas, back panels for response across the rest of Pune and PCMC.

On timing, Pune has two clear peaks: the June–August college admission season, when education advertisers take a lot of inventory, and the Diwali retail run-up. Inventory on the busiest corridors commits several weeks ahead of both, so book early if your campaign has to land in season.
